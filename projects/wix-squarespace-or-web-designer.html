Wix or Squarespace vs a web designer

I build websites for a living, so you might expect me to say never use a website builder. That isn’t my answer. Here’s when they make sense, and when they cost you more than they save.

By John Bell, freelance web and graphic designer in Birmingham · October 2026

The short answer

If you have more time than money and enjoy doing it yourself, Wix or Squarespace can get you online for typically £10 to £40 a month. If you rely on your website for work and would rather spend your evenings on the business, a freelance web designer usually costs £500 to £3,000 once, plus hosting. My websites start from £500.

What website builders are good at

  • A low start. No big bill up front, just a monthly fee.
  • Speed. Pick a template and you can have something live in a weekend.
  • Edit it yourself. Change words and photos whenever you like.
  • Everything in one place. Hosting, security and updates are handled for you.
  • Add-ons for bookings, shops and email lists, if you’re on the right plan.

For someone testing an idea, or a hobby that might become a business, that’s a good deal.

Where they fall short

  • Your time is the real cost. Choosing, writing, resizing photos and fiddling with layouts on a phone takes far longer than most people expect.
  • A good template isn’t a good website. Templates look great with the designer’s photos and words. Filled with your own, often in a hurry, they rarely look the same.
  • You still have to write it. The builder gives you boxes. It doesn’t tell you what to put in them to get people to call.
  • The fee never stops. The features most businesses need, like your own web address without adverts, or taking bookings, usually sit on the paid plans, and the price tends to go up as you add things.
  • It’s hard to leave. You can’t simply pick up a Wix or Squarespace site and move it somewhere else. If you leave, the design usually has to be rebuilt.
  • Google set-up is down to you. The tools are there, but page titles, descriptions and pages for your towns only help if someone fills them in properly.

What a web designer gives you

A designer’s job isn’t only to make it look nice. It’s to work out what your customers need to see, in what order, so they get in touch. With me, that means:

  • A site designed around your business, not a template you’ve squeezed yourself into
  • Pages laid out to lead to a call, a quote request or a booking
  • Designed for phones first, and built to load quickly
  • Set up for Google from day one, with search pages for your main services and towns included
  • Help with your words, so you’re not staring at a blank page
  • Someone to message when something needs changing

Which should you choose?

A website builder is a good fit if:

  • You’re testing an idea and might not carry on
  • Your budget is very tight right now
  • You enjoy design and have the time to do it well
  • Most of your work comes from word of mouth, and the site is just somewhere to point people

A web designer is a better fit if:

  • You want the website to bring in new enquiries, not just sit there
  • You’re up against local competitors on Google
  • Your time is worth more spent on paying work
  • You want the site to match your logo and the rest of your brand

Questions people ask

Is Wix good enough for a small business?

It can be, especially if you’re starting out, have time to do it well and your work mostly comes from word of mouth. If you need the site to win new customers from Google, a designed site usually does more.

Is Squarespace better than Wix?

Both are solid. Squarespace’s templates are cleaner and stricter, while Wix lets you move things anywhere, which also makes it easier to end up with a messy page. Try both and pick the one you find easier.

Can a Wix or Squarespace site rank on Google?

Yes, it can. What matters most is the words on the page, a page for each service and area, proper titles and descriptions, speed on phones and good reviews, not which tool built it.

Can you move my Wix site to a proper website?

Yes. I design a new site, keep the words and photos that work, and move everything across carefully. Your domain can usually come with you.

How much does a web designer cost compared with Wix?

Wix and Squarespace typically cost £10 to £40 a month. My websites start from £500, plus hosting from £10 a month. Over three years the gap is often smaller than people expect.
