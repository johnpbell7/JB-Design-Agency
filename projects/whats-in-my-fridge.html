An app that knows what’s in the fridge.

What’s in my Fridge is my own web app. You photograph your shopping or the receipt, it keeps track of what you’ve got and what needs using first, and it suggests dinners from what’s already there. I designed it and built it, from the logo to the code.

The idea

I kept buying things I already had and throwing away food I’d forgotten about. I wanted one place that knew what was in the fridge, the freezer and the cupboards, without me typing it all in, and that could tell me what to cook from it.

What I did
  • Designed it for one hand on a phone, with a tab bar and sheets that slide up
  • Made the camera the main way in, so a photo or a receipt fills the list
  • Put whatever needs using first at the top, in amber and red
  • Built it in React as a web app you add to your home screen
  • Used Claude to read photos and receipts and suggest meals, Supabase for accounts and Stripe for payments
  • Drew the logo, the icons and the opening animation

01Scan your shopping

A photo does the typing.

Nobody wants to type in a weekly shop, so I made the camera the main way to add food. You photograph the shopping on the worktop, or the shelves of the fridge, and the app lists what it can see.

  • Everything comes back as a suggestion. You tick, rename or change it before anything is saved.
  • If it isn’t sure about something, it says so with a small “not sure” flag.
  • Auto files each item where it usually lives: milk in the fridge, peas in the freezer, rice in the cupboard.

02Or the receipt

Till receipts, read line by line.

Receipts are printed in shorthand, so I had the app turn each line back into a proper name. It works through the whole receipt and leaves out anything you can’t put in a cupboard.

  • “WHL MILK 4PT” becomes Whole milk, and “FF CHKN THIGHS” becomes Chicken thighs.
  • Carrier bags, totals and payment lines are skipped.
  • It reads the date off the receipt, so freshness counts from the day you shopped, not the day you scanned it.

03Use it up

What’s going off comes first.

The list is sorted by what needs eating soonest. I used amber for anything with a couple of days left and red for anything past its date, and kept everything else calm so the warnings stand out.

  • You don’t have to type a date. Freshness is counted from the day it went in, using a typical shelf life for that kind of food.
  • Tap Used and it leaves the list. The snowflake moves it to the freezer instead.
  • Tins, jars and spices just say In stock, so they never nag.

04The usuals

It notices what you always buy.

Once something has turned up on three separate days, the app treats it as one of your usuals. When you run out, it tells you, and one tap puts it on the shopping list.

  • “Running low on the usuals” sits at the top of My food, with Add and a cross to dismiss.
  • Ticking something off the list files it straight back into the fridge, so there’s no second step to forget.
  • You can pin anything you always want in stock, or tell it something isn’t a usual.

05What’s for dinner?

Dinner ideas from what you’ve already got.

Ask what you can make and you get a few real meals built around what’s in stock, starting with whatever needs using. Each one shows what it uses and the one or two things you’d still need to buy.

  • Tap a missing ingredient to put it on the shopping list.
  • Save the meals you like, or open a step-by-step method.
  • Tonight works the same way from a photo of whatever’s on the worktop.

06On your home screen

A web app that opens like an app.

I built it as a web app rather than for the app stores, so there’s nothing to download. You add it to your home screen from the browser and it opens full screen, with its own icon and a short opening animation while your food loads.

  • Your food is saved on the phone first, so the list still opens in a shop with no signal.
  • With an account, it syncs between your devices.
  • The first time you open it on a phone, the app shows you how to add it, step by step.
